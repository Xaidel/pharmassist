# 02 — Competitive landscape: medicine price, stock, and online purchase in the Philippines

- **Ticket:** `https://github.com/Xaidel/pharmassist/issues/2`
- **Researched:** 19 October 2026
- **Beachhead:** Naga City, Camarines Sur
- **Method:** primary sources only — company product pages, store locators, terms and conditions, help centres, and app-store listings. Search engines were used only to locate primary pages.
- **Evidence labels:** **Confirmed** means the claim is stated on a primary page. **Inferred** means it is a reasoned conclusion from primary pages, not a direct statement. Every claim carries a source ID from the table at the end.

## Executive summary

The Philippine market is **single-seller and transaction-first**, not comparison-first. Watsons, Generika, MedsGo, and MedExpress publish their own catalogue prices; Mercury Drug deliberately does not publish prices online. Telehealth players bundle pharmacy delivery through one partner. General marketplaces show prices but prohibit prescription-only medicines. No player offers a cross-pharmacy, local-shelf-price view.

Naga City is not digitally underserved by chain *stores* — Mercury Drug lists 10 branches, Watsons has a store at SM City Naga, and Generika lists at least two branches. It is underserved by **cross-pharmacy price and stock transparency**: every digital price source is a single retailer's own catalogue, and same-day/on-demand pharmacy delivery is either absent or unconfirmed.

The gap Pharmassist fills is therefore not "online pharmacy" — that space is crowded — but **"where in Naga is my full basket cheapest and in stock right now?"** before any purchase, prescription upload, or account is required.

## Confirmed vs inferred

**Confirmed from primary sources**

- Naga store presence: Mercury Drug lists 10 Naga branches; Watsons has a store at SM City Naga with Click & Collect; Generika lists two Naga branches [S8][S12][S16][S17].
- Single-chain price visibility: Watsons, Generika, MedsGo, and MedExpress show prices; Mercury does not publish prices and emails the cost after an order form [S3][S7][S14][S18][S24].
- Prescription gates: Watsons and Generika require Rx upload at checkout; MedsGo requires a prescription or teleconsult; Shopee prohibits prescription-only and pharmacy-only medicines; Meta's commerce policy prohibits medical products [S4][S13][S19][S31][S33].
- Delivery constraints: Watsons express delivery excludes Naga; MedExpress has no Camarines Sur branch; GrabMart's Help Centre coverage list omits Naga [S13][S25][S35].

**Inferred, not directly confirmed**

- Whether MedsGo's nationwide courier claim translates into reliable Naga delivery (the company says “anywhere in the Philippines”; no city-level test).
- Whether Watsons standard delivery reaches a specific Naga address (policy says nationwide except unspecified logistics exclusions; the Naga store is confirmed).
- Whether Shopee and Lazada deliver to Naga (national marketplace model; no city-level checkout test).
- Whether GrabMart is live in Naga, Camarines Sur (Grab's own pages conflict and “Naga” is ambiguous between Cebu and Camarines Sur).
- Whether KonsultaMD's pharmacy serves Naga (no public coverage list; terms say availability may vary).
- The prevalence and behaviour of informal Facebook/Viber sellers in Naga (no primary inventory data).
- Monetisation details for marketplaces and on-demand platforms (not published on the pages reviewed).

## 1. MedsGo (medsgo.ph)

**Model and coverage.** MedsGo describes itself as a “nationwide pharmacy chain” with online ordering, physical branches, and free teleconsultation [S1]. Its branch catalogue, however, lists only Metro Manila locations — Caloocan, Valenzuela, Makati, Mandaluyong, Malabon, Quezon City, Taguig, and Las Piñas [S2]. There is **no Naga City branch**.

**Price transparency.** Prices and “In stock” labels are visible on category pages without signing in, and products can be added to a cart [S3]. This is genuine pre-purchase price transparency, but only for MedsGo's own inventory.

**Prescription handling.** The catalogue separates prescription and over-the-counter categories [S1]. The terms state that customers without a valid prescription may obtain prescription medicines after a consultation and diagnosis by a MedsGo physician, and that MedsGo is the sole seller — “There are no third-party sellers on the Platform” [S4]. A consultation-only visit costs ₱450 [S4].

**Delivery.** MedsGo claims delivery “anywhere in the Philippines” through logistics partners J&T Express and LBC, with free delivery over ₱800 and 2-hour express delivery in Metro Manila [S1][S5]. For Naga, this means **courier delivery is claimed, but there is no local branch or pickup point and no published same-day service** (Inferred from [S1][S2][S5]).

**Monetisation.** Product margin, delivery fees, and a ₱450 consult-only fee; free teleconsultation appears to be an acquisition channel [S1][S4].

**Why it matters.** MedsGo is the closest national analogue to a transparent online pharmacy, but it is a **single-seller store**, not a comparison service. Its Naga presence is a shipping promise, not a local footprint.

## 2. Mercury Drug (mercurydrug.com)

**Model and coverage.** Mercury Drug is a national chain and the most important incumbent in Naga. Its own store locator lists **10 Naga City branches**: Abella, Avenue Square, Concepcion Grande, Elias Angeles, Gen. Luna, J. Miranda, Nagaland E-Mall, P. Burgos, Peñafrancia, and Puregold [S8].

**Price transparency.** Mercury does **not** publish a product catalogue or prices online. “Order Online” is a form where the customer types the items, strength, and form; the site then says: “You will receive an email with the cost of items and a link to pay for your order” [S7]. The form also warns: “Prices subject to change without prior notice” [S7]. A patient therefore cannot compare Mercury's prices before committing to an order, and cannot see stock at a specific Naga branch.

**Prescription handling.** The order form states: “Note that doctor's prescription is required for prescription medicines” [S7].

**Fulfilment.** Mercury offers six options under Gamot Padala: online order for pickup, call-order pickup, call-order delivery, order-for-beneficiary pickup at another branch, curbside pickup, and drive-thru [S9]. It says a full order can be served in “any of more than 1,000 stores nationwide” [S9]. Call-order delivery has a delivery fee starting at ₱100 and accepts e-wallet or debit/credit card [S10]. No Naga-specific delivery SLA or coverage map is published (Inferred from [S9][S10]).

**Monetisation.** Product margin, Suki loyalty programme, and delivery fees [S6][S10].

**Why it matters.** The chain with the widest confirmed Naga footprint in this review is the **least price-transparent online**. That is the clearest evidence that the market's gap is not “another online pharmacy” but **visibility into local chain prices and stock**.

## 3. Watsons (watsons.com.ph)

**Model and coverage.** Watsons is a health-and-beauty chain with an online store and pharmacy category. Its own store page confirms a **Watsons SM City Naga** store at GF, SM City Naga Mall Area, Brgy. Triangulo, Naga City, open 10:00–21:00 daily, offering Click & Collect [S12].

**Price transparency.** Watsons shows prices for both OTC and prescription items without ordering. Examples from its Health and RX pages: Watsons Generics Metformin 500 mg at ₱4.00 per tablet, Amlodipine 5 mg at ₱7.50 per tablet, and Losartan 50 mg at ₱13.25 per tablet [S14]. This is the strongest chain-level price transparency in the set.

**Prescription handling.** Watsons requires a valid prescription to be uploaded during order creation for prescription medicines, including Click & Collect orders [S13]. Its prescription category contains over 2,000 SKUs [S14].

**Delivery.** Watsons delivers nationwide with the exception of “some areas outside our logistics partners' delivery zones”; standard delivery is 3–5 days for the rest of Luzon [S13]. **Express delivery is limited to Metro Manila, Cavite, Rizal, Bulacan, and Laguna — Naga is not included** [S13]. Click & Collect is free, has no minimum purchase, and is available only with credit-card payment [S13]. For Naga, the realistic options are standard courier or pickup at SM City Naga; there is no express delivery (Confirmed for express exclusion; Inferred for standard serviceability).

**Monetisation.** Product margin, Watsons Club membership, and shipping fees/discounts [S11][S13].

**Why it matters.** Watsons proves that a chain can publish Rx prices and offer local pickup, but it still only compares **Watsons to Watsons**. Its Naga pickup is a strong must-match expectation for Pharmassist.

## 4. Generika (generika.com.ph)

**Model and coverage.** Generika is a generic-focused chain owned by Ayala Healthcare Holdings (AC Health) [S19]. Its store pages confirm at least two Naga City branches: **CMS Naga City SF** at 622 Peñafrancia Ave., San Francisco, and **CAMSUR Naga LCC** at Villa Grande Homes Subd., Concepcion Grande [S16][S17].

**Price transparency.** The Generika eStore lists prices publicly. At the time of access it showed 643 results, including 4Fever Paracetamol 125 mg/60 mL at ₱49.75 and Accu-Chek Active strips (25s) at ₱499.00 [S18]. This is a generic-heavy, price-led catalogue.

**Prescription handling.** Generika's FAQ states: “A valid prescription is required before prescription medicines can be dispensed and the order processed,” and the customer must upload or submit it when requested [S19].

**Delivery.** The eStore delivers nationwide, subject to address serviceability and courier availability. Estimated delivery is 2–5 business days for Greater Metro Manila and Luzon; payment methods may include COD, GCash, Maya, card, bank transfer, and MedPadala depending on the order [S19].

**Channel inconsistency.** The main Generika site's “SHOP NOW” button currently points to an “E-Commerce Maintenance Announcement” page that redirects shoppers to Shopee, Lazada, and TikTok Shop, while `store.generika.com.ph` remains live [S15][S20]. That is a trust and discoverability problem for a national chain.

**Monetisation.** Product margin, franchising, GeneriKard loyalty, and MedPadala gift certificates [S15][S19].

**Why it matters.** Generika is the price-led chain most likely to be a Pharmassist partner or benchmark, and it already publishes generic prices. But its online channel is single-chain and currently sends mixed signals about where to shop.

## 5. mWell (mwell.ph / mwell.com.ph)

**Model and coverage.** mWell is Metro Pacific Health's telehealth and wellness app. It offers doctor consultations, e-prescriptions, an e-shop, and medicine delivery through **MedExpress** [S21][S22]. Its own page says: “Have your medicines delivered to your doorstep … via MedExpress. MedExpress representative to confirm order in 30 minutes” [S22]. Its ePharmacy page markets “ePrescription Purchase and Medicine Packages” and “Order prescription medicines for home delivery” [S23].

**Price transparency.** mWell itself does not publish a pharmacy catalogue or prices. Its partner **MedExpress** does: the MedExpress site shows featured products with prices (for example, Biogesic 500 mg at ₱5.56/tablet) and says it shows “real time inventory based on the delivery location you identify or the branch you choose to pick up” [S24].

**Prescription handling.** mWell markets delivery “right after your consultation” using an e-prescription [S22]. MedExpress has a prescription-medicines category, but its public terms do not describe the upload/verification flow in detail [S24][S27]. Treat the exact Rx verification step as **not publicly documented**.

**Delivery.** MedExpress's terms and FAQs describe delivery within 24–48 hours of order confirmation, a minimum order of ₱250, a delivery charge, and out-of-coverage delivery via a partner courier [S26][S27].

**Coverage in Naga.** MedExpress's store locator lists branches in Metro Manila, Agusan del Norte, Bataan, Batangas, Benguet, Bohol, Cebu, Davao, Davao del Norte, Laguna, Misamis Oriental, Negros Oriental, Nueva Ecija, Pangasinan, Quezon, Rizal, South Cotabato, Tarlac, and Zamboanga del Sur — **no Camarines Sur or Naga branch** [S25]. mWell's teleconsultation may be available nationwide, but its medicine delivery is **not confirmed for Naga**. MedExpress's terms allow out-of-coverage delivery via a partner courier with bank-deposit payment, so a Naga order may be possible, but there is no local branch or published same-day service [S27].

**Monetisation.** Consultation fees, health plans (for example, a ₱399 Healthsavers plan), and partner services; pharmacy fulfilment economics are not disclosed [S21][S22].

**Why it matters.** mWell is a telehealth funnel into a single pharmacy partner. It does not compare pharmacies, and its delivery footprint has no Naga branch; Naga service is unconfirmed.

## 6. KonsultaMD (konsulta.md)

**Model and coverage.** KonsultaMD is a telehealth platform by Metro Pacific Health Tech Corporation. Its current site says it offers “24/7 Online Doctor Consultations, 24/7 Pharmacy, Diagnostics and Home Care” and lists “Convenient Medicine Delivery” as a benefit [S28]. Its terms describe medicine delivery as one of the services and define a licensed pharmacy as a possible Healthcare Provider [S29].

**Domain note.** `konsultamd.com` is now a parked GoDaddy lander; the official site is `konsulta.md` [S28][S40]. This matters for citation hygiene and for patients searching the brand.

**Price transparency.** There is **no public pharmacy catalogue or price list**. The pharmacy appears to be app-gated; the site only advertises the capability [S28][S30]. A patient cannot compare prices before registering and entering the app (Inferred from absence of a public catalogue).

**Prescription handling.** Not publicly documented beyond the existence of consultations, e-prescriptions, and pharmacy services [S28][S29].

**Coverage in Naga.** Not published. The terms explicitly say pharmacy and diagnostics “availability may vary and is not guaranteed” [S29]. Naga coverage is therefore **unknown**, not confirmed.

**Monetisation.** Health plans and subscriptions (for example, a Family Annual Health Plan at ₱1,999 for 60 video-consult credits), consultation fees, and pharmacy/diagnostics services [S28][S29].

**Why it matters.** KonsultaMD is a telehealth bundle, not a price-comparison product. Its pharmacy is opaque by design and cannot be relied on for Naga-specific stock or price checks.

## 7. Shopee (shopee.ph)

**Model and coverage.** Shopee is a general marketplace with visible listings and prices. It is a national platform; Naga buyers can order through seller/courier delivery (Inferred from the platform model; no city-level checkout test was performed).

**Price transparency.** Yes — listings show prices, variants, and seller information before ordering. But the price is the seller's price, not a local pharmacy's shelf price.

**Prescription handling.** Shopee's current **Prohibited and Restricted Items Policy** explicitly bans “Drugs, prescription-only medicines, pharmacy-only medicines, drug-like substances and associated paraphernalia” [S31]. This is the decisive constraint: Shopee is not a compliant channel for prescription-medicine comparison or dispensing.

**Delivery.** Marketplace courier delivery; not a local pharmacy stock or pickup service (Inferred).

**Monetisation.** Marketplace commissions, advertising, and payment/logistics services (Inferred; not stated on the policy page).

**Why it matters.** Shopee shows that Filipinos will buy health products online at transparent prices, but it **is not a compliant Rx layer under its own policy**. That leaves OTC and supplements, which are not the core of a prescription basket.

## 8. Lazada (lazada.com.ph)

**Model and coverage.** Lazada is a general marketplace with visible listings and prices, and nationwide courier delivery (Inferred for Naga; no city-level checkout test was performed).

**Price transparency.** Yes — listings show prices before ordering.

**Prescription handling.** Lazada's public help centre did not expose a readable prohibited-items page in this session; its seller “guidelines on restricted categories” page is access-gated [S32]. The 2019 FDA order named both Shopee and Lazada in a cease-and-desist order on online medicine sales [S39]. Treat Lazada as **restricted for Rx medicines and not a compliant comparison/dispensing channel**; confirm the current rule in issue #1.

**Delivery.** Marketplace courier delivery; not local pharmacy stock (Inferred).

**Monetisation.** Marketplace commissions, advertising, and payment/logistics services (Inferred).

**Why it matters.** Same as Shopee: useful for OTC and supplements, structurally unable to solve the prescription-price-comparison problem.

## 9. GrabMart and GrabHealth (grab.com)

**Model.** GrabMart is an on-demand grocery and essentials marketplace that includes “over-the-counter pharmacy supplies” [S34]. GrabHealth is explicitly “Delivery of medicines and adjacent services via GrabMart” [S36]. This is OTC/health essentials, not a prescription pharmacy.

**Price transparency.** In-app per-store prices, visible before ordering [S34].

**Coverage in Naga — conflicting evidence.** The GrabMart marketing page lists “Naga” among its service areas [S34]. Grab's Help Centre article “What is GrabMart?”, updated 15 October 2026, lists Metro Manila, Greater Metro Manila, Cebu, Pampanga, Davao, Batangas, Bacolod, Iloilo, and Dumaguete — **it omits Naga** [S35]. Grab's general locations page also lists “Naga” [S37], but that page covers all Grab services, not GrabMart specifically. Because “Naga” could mean Naga City, Cebu or Naga City, Camarines Sur, **GrabMart availability in Naga, Camarines Sur is unconfirmed** and should be tested in the app before the PRD treats it as a competitor or a channel.

**Prescription handling.** None — OTC/health essentials only [S34][S36].

**Monetisation.** Delivery fees and merchant commissions (Inferred).

**Why it matters.** If GrabMart is live in Naga, it sets a convenience benchmark (within-the-hour delivery) but still cannot handle Rx or cross-pharmacy comparison. If it is not live, Naga's on-demand gap is even wider.

## 10. Facebook and Viber seller groups

**Model.** Facebook Pages, Groups, and Marketplace, plus Viber Communities, are used by informal sellers and some local pharmacies to post stock, prices, and delivery offers. Viber Communities is a group-chat feature with unlimited members, admin controls, and hidden phone numbers — not a marketplace with catalogues or checkout [S38].

**Price transparency.** Prices are posted ad hoc in posts, comments, or chats; there is no structured catalogue, pack-size normalisation, or stock timestamp (Inferred).

**Prescription handling.** No platform-level prescription verification. Facebook's Commerce Policies prohibit “medical and healthcare products and services, including medical devices or ingestible supplements” in commerce content, and Facebook Marketplace restricts businesses in the Philippines [S33]. Informal groups may still post such content, but it is against policy and unverified (Inferred from [S33]).

**Coverage in Naga.** Facebook and Viber are widely used in the Philippines, and local seller groups are common; however, **specific Naga groups could not be verified from primary sources** in this session. Treat availability as high but unmeasured (Inferred).

**Delivery.** Seller-arranged meet-up, local delivery, or courier; no escrow or platform guarantee (Inferred).

**Monetisation.** Seller-set price; no platform transaction fee for ordinary group posts (Inferred).

**Why it matters.** This is the real “competitor” for independent pharmacies: informal, relationship-based, and invisible to search. Pharmassist's opportunity is to formalise that inventory without destroying the relationship.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **Price transparency is single-chain.** Watsons, Generika, MedsGo, and MedExpress publish their own prices. Mercury does not publish prices at all. None publishes a competitor's price [S3][S7][S14][S18][S24].
2. **Online stock is not local shelf stock.** MedExpress is the closest to branch-level real-time inventory, but it is one chain and has no Naga branch [S24][S25]. Chain e-commerce stock does not tell a patient what is on the shelf at SM City Naga or Panganiban.
3. **Prescription is a wall, not a comparison layer.** Watsons and Generika require Rx upload at checkout [S13][S19]; MedsGo requires a prescription or consult [S4]; Shopee bans Rx outright [S31]; Facebook's commerce policy bans medical products [S33]. No one lets a patient compare Rx prices across pharmacies before uploading.
4. **Naga has chain stores but thin digital/pharmacy delivery.** Mercury has 10 branches, Watsons has a Naga store with Click & Collect, and Generika has at least two branches [S8][S12][S16][S17]. MedsGo has no Naga branch, MedExpress has no Camarines Sur branch, Watsons express delivery excludes Naga, and GrabMart's Naga coverage is unconfirmed [S2][S13][S25][S34][S35].
5. **Independent pharmacies are effectively invisible.** They are not in chain catalogues or store locators, and their Facebook/Viber presence is unstructured. This is the largest unserved inventory pool in Naga (Inferred).
6. **The market is segmented by transaction type.** Chains sell their own stock; marketplaces sell OTC/supplements; telehealth sells consultations plus a single pharmacy partner; on-demand sells convenience. None sells **local price and stock information as a standalone decision tool**.

## The gap Pharmassist fills

Pharmassist's map-first, cross-pharmacy, single-basket comparison fills six specific gaps:

1. **Cross-pharmacy comparison.** No existing product shows the same medicine across multiple Naga pharmacies. Every price source is a single seller.
2. **Local shelf price and stock.** E-commerce prices are chain/warehouse prices, not the price at the branch a patient can walk into. A map-first view with branch-level prices and stock is new.
3. **Single basket across pharmacies.** A patient with a multi-item prescription currently has to call or visit multiple stores. No product lets them build one basket, see which pharmacy has which items, and compare the total.
4. **Pre-prescription browsing.** Rx upload is a gate at every licensed player. Pharmassist can let patients compare before uploading, then hand off to the pharmacy for verification and dispensing.
5. **Independent pharmacy inclusion.** Chains have store locators; independents have Facebook/Viber. A structured, map-first catalogue gives independents a digital shelf without requiring a full e-commerce build.
6. **Naga-specific, cash-first, pickup-aware design.** The national players optimise for Metro Manila express delivery. Naga patients need pickup, walking distance, store hours, cash/GCash, and Senior/PWD pricing.

## Implications for Pharmassist

### Differentiation

- **Own the question, not the transaction.** “Where in Naga is my full prescription cheapest and in stock?” is unserved. Do not compete with Watsons/Generika on checkout or with Grab on delivery.
- **Map-first, branch-level truth.** Show the pharmacy, branch, distance, hours, price, stock status, and last-verified timestamp. This is the opposite of a warehouse catalogue.
- **Single basket, split fulfilment.** Let the patient see that Pharmacy A has 3 items and Pharmacy B has 2, with total cost and pickup/delivery implications. No incumbent does this.
- **No forced Rx upload to browse.** Compare first; upload only at handoff. This is a privacy and conversion advantage over Watsons/Generika/MedsGo.
- **Include independents.** The long tail is invisible online; a lightweight claim/update flow can make Pharmassist the only place that shows them.
- **No pay-to-rank.** Keep organic cheapest/nearest ranking credible; sponsored placement must be clearly labelled and capped (per standing PRD decisions).

### Risks

- **Price and stock staleness.** Shelf prices and stock change daily. A wrong price destroys trust faster than a missing price. Require timestamps, confidence levels, a “report outdated” path, and pharmacy confirmation before dispensing.
- **Pharmacy cooperation and price-war fear.** Some pharmacies will not want competitors to see their prices. Neutral ranking, no pay-to-rank, and a clear “we send you patients” value proposition are essential. Ticket 06 should test willingness.
- **Regulatory limits.** Publishing prescription-medicine prices, advertising rules, Rx image handling, and Data Privacy Act obligations may constrain the product. Ticket 01 must settle this before the PRD promises Rx price comparison.
- **Big-chain response.** Mercury could publish prices; Watsons could extend express delivery; Grab could add Naga. Pharmassist's defence is local breadth (independents) and cross-pharmacy neutrality, not speed of delivery.
- **Marketplace/telehealth bundling.** Shopee/Lazada already own OTC traffic; mWell/KonsultaMD own telehealth. Pharmassist should integrate or hand off, not replicate.
- **Thin unit economics in a small city.** 50 medicines × 10 pharmacies is a manual pilot. The PRD must budget collection time, verification, and pharmacy support, not assume real-time data.
- **“Lowest price” liability.** Show total cost including delivery/pickup and discounts; avoid unverifiable superlatives.

### Must-match expectations (table stakes)

- Prices visible **without login** and **without ordering**.
- Stock status with a **last-verified timestamp** and a clear “confirm with pharmacy” caveat.
- Search by **brand and generic**, with strength, dosage form, and pack size; show **per-unit price** where possible.
- **Map + list** toggle with distance, store hours, and contact/directions.
- **Basket split across pharmacies** with item availability and total cost.
- **Rx upload only at checkout/handoff**, with immediate discard and no server-side storage (per standing PRD decisions).
- **Senior/PWD discount** indication and **pickup vs delivery** cost comparison.
- **GCash/COD** and local pickup options.
- **Report outdated price/stock** and a visible freshness indicator.
- **No clinical advice**, dosage guidance, or diagnosis.
